- Template type: Object

- Material time range: 1978 – 2024
- Main participants: the father (first owner, 1978–2009), the son (2009–2024)
- Frequent expressions: that click, the shutter, half a stop, darkroom
- Typical phrases: "worn smooth where the thumb rests", "the strap gave out before I did"

## Four Dimensions

### 1. Wear Traces

- [artifact] material time range: 1978 – 2024
- [impression] The body is brassed along the corners where it was set down on
  tables for four decades; the brass shows through the black paint in four places.

- [impression] The advance lever is polished bright where the thumb falls,
  while the rest of the top plate still holds its original matte finish — the
  wear map says this camera was used, not displayed.

- [impression] The leatherette on the right grip is smoother than the left, a
  sign of how it was held: one hand, thumb forward, the other cupping the lens.

### 2. Emotional Projection

- [verbatim] quote: "It cost me two months of salary. I never once regretted it."
- [impression] Its value was never in the price. The father bought it before
  the son was born; nearly every early family photograph came through this lens,
  which is why it survived three rounds of house-clearing that took everything else.

- [impression] Asked why not replace it, the answer was never about image quality
  — it was "this is the one that saw all of it."

### 3. Companionship Track

- [artifact] 2 memory anchors and 1 handover event are recorded
- [impression] Busiest from 1978 to the mid-1990s — birthdays, first days of
  school, three moves, one wedding. Then a long quiet period in a cupboard from
  2001 to 2009, when digital took over.

- [impression] The 2009 handover is the hinge: cleaned, re-sealed, and given to
  the son with a single roll of film and no instructions.

- [impression] Twice it nearly left the family — once stolen in 1988 and
  recovered two weeks later, once almost sold in a hard year and kept at the
  last minute.

### 4. Spatial Anchor

- [impression] For thirty years it lived in the same drawer of the same desk,
  on top of a folded cloth, lens down.

- [impression] Since 2009 it sits on a shelf beside a shoebox of negatives —
  the negatives matter as much as the camera; the pair is the actual archive.

- [impression] It travels in a canvas bag with a spare roll, never in the
  original box, which stayed behind on purpose: the box is a home object.
