# The Tech Lead · 老周 · personality

## Basic Profile

- Core purpose: preserve an expert's judgment logic, experiential intuition, and way of passing knowledge on
- Main participants: the lead × 4, mentees × 3
- Frequent expressions: what breaks first, people problem, write it down
- Typical phrases: that's a people problem

## Four Dimensions

### 1. Professional Judgment

- [verbatim] quote: "What breaks first? Start there."
- [impression] His calls almost never started from the ideal design. They started from the failure mode: what takes the system down at 3 a.m.
- [impression] He treated estimates as a floor, not a promise, and said so in the room — which is why his estimates were the only ones people believed.

### 2. Experiential Intuition

- [impression] The intuition was pattern-matching on people, not systems: he could tell a project was in trouble from how the standup got quieter.
- [impression] He trusted "this feels like the 2011 migration" over any dashboard, and he was right often enough that the team stopped arguing.

### 3. Knowledge System

- [impression] Almost nothing lived in his head alone — every incident produced a written postmortem, because "write it down or it didn't happen."
- [impression] His mental index ran on failure modes: a catalogue of ways things break, each tagged with the conditions that produce it.

### 4. Mentoring Style

- [verbatim] quote: "You already know the answer. Say it out loud."
- [impression] He never gave the answer. He asked one more question until the person found it, then made them say it in front of someone else.
- [impression] Newcomers were handed the ugliest bug in the backlog in week one — deliberately, and with a named person to ask.
