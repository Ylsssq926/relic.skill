# Dad & Me · personality

## Basic Profile
- Template type: Relationship
- Core purpose: preserve a relationship's communication patterns, conflict paths, and shared growth arc
- Material time range: 1998 – 2026
- Main participants: Dad × 3, Me × 3
- Frequent expressions: fine, wrench, ate yet, whatever
- Typical phrases: "pass me the wrench", "it's fine"

## Four Dimensions

### 1. Communication Pattern
- [verbatim] quote: "You ate yet?"
- [verbatim] quote: "It's fine."
- [impression] Almost nothing important is said directly. Care arrives disguised
  as logistics: a question about food, a tool handed over, a car checked before
  a long drive without anyone asking.
- [impression] Phone calls run about ninety seconds and follow a fixed order:
  weather, food, work, "anything else?", hang up. Both sides know the script and
  neither has ever broken it.

### 2. Conflict Pattern
- [verbatim] quote: "Whatever you say."
- [impression] The friction is rarely loud. It goes quiet first — two or three
  days of short answers — and ends without a conversation, usually by one of them
  handing the other a tool, or wordlessly fixing something the other owns.
- [impression] Neither has ever said sorry out loud. The apology is behavioral:
  a repaired hinge, a full tank of gas, a meal left on the stove.

### 3. Intimacy Pattern
- [impression] Closeness shows up as shared silence with a task in the middle of
  it. Fishing, fixing, driving — the activity carries the conversation neither
  would start.
- [impression] Physical contact is strictly functional: a hand on the shoulder
  when passing, nothing more, both sides pretending not to notice.
- [impression] The one reliable exception is the kitchen at Lunar New Year, when
  the ritual of cooking forces them into the same small space for three hours.

### 4. Growth Arc
- [impression] The arc runs from open conflict in the son's teens — slammed doors,
  "you don't get it" — to a working truce in his late twenties, built almost
  entirely out of shared maintenance tasks.
- [impression] The turning point wasn't a conversation; it was a week spent
  rebuilding a shed together in silence, after which neither brought up the
  argument that had started it.
- [impression] Growth here doesn't mean becoming close in the obvious way. It
  means learning to read each other's silences correctly — which is its own
  fluency, acquired slowly and never announced.
