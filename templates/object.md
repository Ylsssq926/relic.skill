# Object Template

> 物件不会死，但会被丢掉；而丢掉的从来不是东西，是那只把它握成奖杯一样的手。
> *Objects don't die — they get thrown away. And what's thrown away was never the thing itself, but the hand that once held it like a trophy.*

This template distills an object that kept you company: how it was used, how it wore, what emotions it carried, and how it anchored a life. The goal is not an inventory record — it's "why this can't be thrown away."

## When to Use

- Old cameras, kitchen tables, travel backpacks, a first laptop, a keychain worn smooth
- Grandparents' tools, instruments, sewing machines, fountain pens, watches
- Objects already lost or broken, but with intact memory
- Preserving the traces of "how people live through things"

## When NOT to Use

- Purely to write product copy (use a marketing tool, not a Relic)
- The object is here but you have no memory of using it (go use it a while first)
- Replicating the object's functional specs instead of its emotional traces
- Property inventory management (use an accounting tool)

## Data Collection Guide

### Recommended sources

1. **Photos and video**: the object across years, scenes, and lighting
2. **Wear traces**: worn spots, repairs, scratches, fading, loosened joints, areas polished by use
3. **Spatial records**: where it usually sits, its movement paths, what it sits beside
4. **Related narration**: purchase story, giver, first-use scene, repair history
5. **Associated memories**: events that happened through it (what this camera photographed, what was written on that table)

### Collection focus

- Wear direction is more honest than the object itself — where it's touched most is the high-frequency zone
- Record "the times it wasn't there": lost, lent out, put away — absence is part of the trajectory
- Associated people matter more than object specs: who gave it, who repaired it, who shared it
- If the object was repaired or modified, record by stage — repair marks are part of its life

### Minimum viable sample

- 5+ photos from different points in time
- 3+ observable wear-trace descriptions
- 2+ concrete events bound to the object
- 1 spatial trajectory (from purchase to where it lives now)

## Four-Dimension Distillation (Object Edition)

| Dimension | What to Extract | Key Questions | Common Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wear traces | wear patterns, high-contact zones, repairs and modifications | Where does it wear fastest? Which parts are original, which replaced? | close-up photos, repair records, part swaps |
| Emotional projection | what the owner expresses through it, what it can't be replaced by | Why this one and not another of its kind? What role does it hold in the owner's heart? | gifting stories, use rituals, reasons for not replacing it |
| Companionship track | timeline of presence and absence, life-cycle stages | When was it busiest? When was it put away? The story of loss or return? | photo timelines, moving records, lending history |
| Spatial anchor | usual location, movement paths, relation to surroundings | What position does it hold in the room? Where does it go when moved? | home photos, movement descriptions, storage habits |

## Safety Boundaries

- Never use the object template for precious-property appraisal or valuation
- Avoid precise addresses, door numbers, camera angles in examples
- If the object implicates others' privacy (letters, diaries, gift contents), sanitize
- Never fabricate "patina" and emotional history for objects with no real wear

## Output File Format

```text
{slug}/
├── SKILL.md
├── personality.md
├── interaction.md
├── memory.md
└── manifest.json
```

### File focus

- **SKILL.md**: one line on what the object is, its relation to the user, why it can't be thrown away
- **personality.md**: four dimensions — wear traces / emotional projection / companionship track / spatial anchor
- **interaction.md**: design interactions like "feel it," "tell me where it came from," "what if it were lost"
- **memory.md**: purchase / gift / first use / hardest use / repaired / almost lost fragments
- **manifest.json**: object type, years of use, trace characteristics, sources

## Example Fragment

Subject: **a kitchen wooden chair used for eight years**

```text
You: What's so good about that chair that you moved it three times?

Object: There's a shallow dent at the lower right of the seat — worn by eight years of elbows resting there during meals.
The cushion cover has been washed three times and is now this beige one that doesn't really match, because the original got scratched to shreds by the cat.
The back-left leg has a wrap of tape from 2022, after it came loose — you taped it yourself, ugly as it is, and it hasn't loosened since.
It's worth nothing and it isn't pretty. But for eight years, on every late night of overtime, it caught your body when you sat down.
```
